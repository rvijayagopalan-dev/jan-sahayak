# JanSahayak - https://jan-sahayak-mu.vercel.app/

JanSahayak is a proposed, citizen-centred platform concept for helping people discover and navigate public services, entitlements, life-event support, mobility, care and other assistance through one connected journey. It is intended to coordinate service discovery and workflows—not replace the institutions responsible for official records, rules, decisions or service delivery.

## Architecture and operations

The concept brings together citizen-facing channels (web, mobile, multilingual voice and assisted services), 24 product domains, and a shared intelligence fabric. The fabric includes identity and consent, service and life-event knowledge, entitlement discovery, legal information, geospatial and mobility support, provider coordination, workflow management, communications, and outcome intelligence.

A versioned integration layer would connect to government and authorized partner systems where documented interfaces and approvals permit. Where an authorized API is unavailable, the platform can direct citizens to official portals or assisted channels. Status should come from permitted authoritative sources, or be clearly identified as citizen-reported or unverified.

Operations are proposed as federated across national, State/UT, district, local-community and service-provider levels. Privacy, security, accessibility, human oversight and auditability are foundational: agents may provide guidance and coordinate workflows, but do not make official eligibility decisions, issue certificates, or take consequential actions without required authorization and human approval.

## Impact and citizen outcomes

JanSahayak's proposed evaluation framework distinguishes activity and platform outputs from verified citizen outcomes. Measures could cover service access and discoverability, completion, time and effort, grievance routing and resolution, inclusion, provider quality, trust, reliability and public value.

Evaluation should establish baselines, define denominators and data sources, account for external dependencies, and use independent review where feasible. Outcomes should be examined across geography, language, age, disability-access needs and channel where lawful and responsible. Public reporting should use aggregated or de-identified data and explain methodology and limitations.

Any targets in the framework are illustrative planning examples only—not measured results or approved government targets. JanSahayak is a concept proposal, not a deployed or approved government service or architecture.
